Governance guardrails : mandatory

These are not optional framing; they are what separates a plausible student demo from something an Indian insurer's risk committee could look at without alarm.

1. Recommend, never decide. No agent auto-approves, auto-repudiates, auto-quotes or auto-binds. IRDAI's outsourcing framework treats underwriting and claims decision-making as core activities that remain the insurer's own responsibility. Building recommend-only is therefore both the safe design and the regulatorily coherent one. Most importantly, the students should be able to explain why.
2. Grounded output or no output. Every coverage conclusion, deduction, appetite decision or medical assertion carries a citation to a retrieved clause, guideline paragraph or source page. A fabricated or misattributed citation is graded as a critical failure, not a minor error.
3. Synthetic data only. No real policyholder or claimant data under any circumstances. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 governs personal data handling, and IRDAI's record-maintenance rules require insurance records to be held in India. Every team generates its own synthetic corpus and documents the generator.
4. Deployment note. Each team submits a one-page analysis: where would this model run in production, what data crosses which boundary, and what changes if the insurer cannot send policyholder data to an overseas API? Teams choosing open-weight or India-hosted models should say so and measure the quality cost. This is a real architectural decision Indian insurers are making right now.
5. Fairness and non-discrimination. Decisions must rest on risk-relevant features and cited rules, never on proxies for religion, caste, region or gender inferred from name, address or PIN code. Teams working on underwriting and appetite (Tracks B and C) must actively test for this and report results.
6. Cite the live text. IRDAI's regulatory position on timelines, moratorium periods and product rules move quickly and have all changed recently. Every regulatory claim in a team's report must cite the current instrument on irdai.gov.in with its date. Working from a stale summary, including this one, is itself a marked error.
7. Human-in-the-loop and escalation design. Define explicitly what triggers escalation, what the human sees, and how disagreement is captured for future improvement.
